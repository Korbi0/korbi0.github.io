
# Bridging the Gap: The Search for a Dual Credence-Probability View in the Sleeping Beauty Problem

## The distinction between credence and objective probability appears in multiple Sleeping Beauty solutions, but no philosopher has explicitly formulated the exact position described. Closest are hybridizing approaches that separate subjective credence from objective chance, particularly Bostrom's model.

The Sleeping Beauty problem has generated intense philosophical debate about probability, rational belief, and self-locating knowledge since Adam Elga's seminal 2000 paper. While the literature contains rich discussions distinguishing between different types of probability judgments, my research indicates that no philosopher has explicitly advocated for the specific position described in the query that cleanly separates credence from belief about objective probability in precisely that way.

The position in question combines these elements:

1. Sleeping Beauty's credence in HEADS upon awakening should be 1/3, change to 1/2 if told it's Monday, and drop to 0 if told it's Tuesday
2. Simultaneously, she should believe the objective probability of HEADS is 1/2 throughout

### Key Approaches That Come Close

**Nick Bostrom's Hybrid Model (2007)** offers the closest match to this position. In "Sleeping Beauty and Self-Location: A Hybrid Model," Bostrom argues that the apparent conflict between halfers and thirders stems from answering different questions. He distinguishes between:

- The objective chance of the coin landing heads (1/2)
- The subjective credence appropriate to a specific awakening (1/3)

Bostrom's framework allows for maintaining that the objective probability is 1/2 while acknowledging that subjective credence should be 1/3, making it compatible with the first part of the desired position.

**The Disambiguation Approach** similarly suggests the Sleeping Beauty question is fundamentally ambiguous between:

- "What is your credence that the coin landed heads in the act of tossing?" (1/2)
- "What is your credence that the coin landed heads in the toss to set up this awakening?" (1/3)

This approach explicitly separates judgments about objective chance from judgments about one's specific awakening situation.

**The De Se vs. De Dicto Distinction** provides another conceptual framework that could support the desired position. This distinction, emphasized in works by David Lewis and others, separates:

- De se beliefs about one's location in the world ("the coin landed heads in this awakening")
- De dicto beliefs about the world itself ("the objective probability of the coin landing heads")

This framework could support holding different probability judgments for these different types of propositions.

### Dynamic Credence Updates

While Elga's thirder position does specify that if Beauty is told it's Monday, her credence in HEADS should update to 1/2, I found no philosophical position that explicitly combines:

1. The full credence dynamics described (1/3 → 1/2 when told Monday, 1/3 → 0 when told Tuesday)
2. A persistent belief that the objective probability remains 1/2 throughout these updates

### Jacob Ross's Analysis

Jacob Ross's work on the Sleeping Beauty problem, particularly "Sleeping Beauty, Countable Additivity, and Rational Dilemmas" (2010), explores tensions between different probability principles relevant to this problem. While Ross doesn't advocate for the specific position described in the query, his analysis helps clarify how different probability judgments can come into conflict in self-locating scenarios.

### Cian Dorr and Frank Arntzenius's Approach

Dorr and Arntzenius proposed a modified version of the Sleeping Beauty problem that helps clarify how information affects credence. Their approach demonstrates that when Beauty eliminates the possibility of it being Tuesday with the coin having landed heads, three equally likely possibilities remain, yielding a credence of 1/3 in heads. This approach may offer resources for understanding how credence updates when Beauty learns what day it is.

## Conclusion

The philosophical literature on the Sleeping Beauty problem contains several approaches that distinguish between different types of probability judgments in ways relevant to the position described in the query. However, no philosopher appears to have explicitly combined all elements of this position—specifically, the dynamics of credence updating from 1/3 to 1/2 (when told it's Monday) while maintaining a stable belief that the objective probability is 1/2.

The closest matches are hybridizing approaches like Bostrom's that explicitly separate judgments about objective chance from judgments about subjective credence in self-locating scenarios. These approaches provide conceptual frameworks that could support the position described, even if they don't articulate it in exactly those terms.

This exploration reveals a potential opportunity in the philosophical literature: to develop a more explicit account of how credence and belief about objective probability can diverge in self-locating scenarios, with credence responding dynamically to new information while beliefs about objective probability remain stable.
