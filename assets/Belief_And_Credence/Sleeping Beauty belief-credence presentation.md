 
# Methodological Behaviourism about Beliefs and Credences

- Sellars: Think of "belief" analogously to a theoretical term in science
	- observational term: Refers to something of which you have non-inferential knowledge
	- theoretical term: Refers to something of which you only have inferential knowledge
- For a methodological behaviourist, any concept that you want to introduce has to be introduced in terms of vocabulary pertaining to overt behviour.
	- In contrast to *philosophical* behaviourism, this leaves open the possibility that some behaviouristically acceptable concepts be introduced as *theoretical* terms, thus allowing talk about entities which go beyond those that can be referred to with purely observational vocabulary
- Phenomenon to be explained: People can sometimes reason successfully without saying their reasoning process out loud
- Theory: Postulate inner episodes, call them "beliefs", which are modelled on overt verbal behaviour but without having to be uttered in any way. What they have in common with explicitly uttered sentences is their semantic properties (e.g. aiming at truth), and their *natural fruition* is overt verbal behaviour, in the form of *assertion*.
- Where *assertion* is a normatively governed activity that takes place in the language game of giving and asking for reasons.
	- you should assert $p$ only if $p$ is true and if you are willing to provide reasons for why $p$ should be true
	- you should retract your assertion if you are presented with strong reasons for $\neg p$
	- if you asserted $p$ and asserted $p \rightarrow q$ then you should also be willing to assert $q$.
- Methodological behaviourism about credences:
	- Ascribe credence $c$ to an agent iff it explains their behaviour via Bayesian decision theory, in particular their betting behaviour.


## Justifications for ascribing beliefs and credences to people
- Beliefs: I should ascribe belief that $p$ to someone else or to myself, roughly speaking, if $p$ is the kind of thing that person would say (or, more precisely, *assert*).
- Credences: I should ascribe credence $c$ in $p$ to someone or to myself if that person would be willing to bet on $p$ at the odds that would be fair according to $c$. (If they would be willing to bet $\frac{c}{1-c}$ of their pounds against one of my pounds on $p$)

- Assertion is a social activity governed by certain norms (which we might call norms of reasonableness)
- Betting (and more generally decision-making) is governed by norms of rationality

|                           | Belief                                                                                                                                           | Credence                |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------- |
| **Purpose**               | Reasoning (socially)                                                                                                                             | Decision Making         |
| **Domain**                | Reasonableness                                                                                                                                   | Rationality             |
| **Norms**                 | Logical coherence; "revise logically incompatible beliefs", "accept logically entailed beliefs", "provide reasons for your beliefs upon request" | Don't be dutch-bookable |
| **Correctness Condition** | Truth                                                                                                                                            | /                       |


## Application to sleeping beauty

If you are woken up, and someone asks you what the probability is that the coin came up "heads", and you say anything else than $\frac{1}{2}$, it is impossible for you to give reasons in support of that claim, since before you were put to sleep, you knew the coin was fair, and upon being woken up, you gain no new information. You are e.g. not entitled to the assertion "The probability is 1/3". You are only entitled to the assertion "The probability is 1/2". Therefore, if you believe anything else, you are being unreasonable.

If you are woken up and someone offers you a bet at even odds that the coin was "heads", you will in expectation lose money if you accept it. If you bet in accordance with a credence of $1/2$, you make yourself susceptible to a dutch book; you would be acting irrationally.
