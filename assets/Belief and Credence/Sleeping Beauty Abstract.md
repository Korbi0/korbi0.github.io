
This paper looks at the "Sleeping Beauty" problem from the perspective of a specific methodologically behaviourist account of belief and credence inspired by John MacFarlane's recent paper "Belief, what is it good for?", and argues that, based on such an account, Sleeping Beauty should *believe that* the probability of "heads" is 1/2, but her *credence in* "heads" should be 1/3.

It does this based on the claim that these two "should"s are different, in the sense of reflecting two different normativities: The normativity of the language game of giving and asking for reasons, governing assertion, which is inherited by belief as that internal state which disposes one to make an assertion; and the normativity of rationality, governing (among other things) betting, which is inherited by credence as that internal state which disposes one to enter into a bet at certain odds.

It argues that Sleeping Beauty cannot give any intersubjectively acceptable reasons for changing her belief from 1/2 on Sunday to 1/3 upon being woken up; that she should therefore not assert and hence not believe it.

Simultaneously, that any Sleeping Beauty whose credence are not 1/3 is susceptible to a Dutch Book; and that it would therefore be irrational to have any such credence.
