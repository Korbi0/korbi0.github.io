- But what is the point of belief if it has no business in rationalizing and explaining behaviour?
- Why does it matter what is or isn't a reason, if that is never gonna have an effect on credence and, therefore, on behaviour?
- Here's a thought: 
	- One thing about belief is that it has *truth* as a correctness condition.
	- And to settle a bet, what matters is whether $p$ is *true*.
	- So when it comes time to settle a bet, we need to figure out whether $p$ is true.
	- At that point, we need to *reason* using our *beliefs*, and in the end, should award the stake of the bet to the person who bet on $p$ iff we *believe* that $p$ (which we should do iff $p$ is true.)
- That would give a connection between belief and credence in the form of: My credence in $p$ reflects the degree to which I expect that I will believe that $p$ is true at the time of the settling of the bet. (With the possible caveat / correction that, strictly speaking, what matters for my credence is not whether *I* will believe $p$ to be true, but whether *whoever is the judge settling the bet* will believe it. This should be identical to myself believing it *iff* I expect the judge and myself to hold the same belief. This expectation can be assumed to be particularly strong if $p$ is empirically verifiable, or if I trust the judge to make the kind of judgment which one needs to make to settle $p$'s truth.)
	- Consider the scenario in which Sleeping Beauty is asked about her credence in $heads$. We need to distinguish the point in time at which Sleeping Beauty would enter into a bet on $heads$ (which I argue she should do at odds of 1:2, corresponding to a credence of 1/3), and the point in time at which the bet would be settled.
	- At the first point in time $t_1$ (just after being woken up), Sleeping Beauty neither believes that the coin landed $heads$ nor that it landed $tails$. She believes that the two are equally likely, since she knows the coin is fair and that is all she knows about the coinflip. The kind of information that would allow one to assert sth like "the coin's probability for $heads$ is not 1/2" is information like "the coin is weighted" or "the coin is a trick coin with $heads$ printed on both sides". She has no such information which could be cited as a reason for a different probability than 1/2. Her credence in $heads$ should be 1/3 because everything else exposes her to a dutch book. In this sense, she expects to see $head$ at $t_2$, when the true outcome of the coinflip is revealed to her, to degree 1/3 (what does it mean thought to expect that to that degree? Should it be cashed out in terms of "among the possible futures she sees for the situation in which the bet is settled and the coin is revealed, 1/3 have $heads$ and 2/3 have $tails$?" Or should I resist any further reduction of credence to truth-abt statements or statements that give the impression of trying to be truth-apt? Probably the latter, right? Credence is just a disposition in your mind and that is all it is.)
	- At $t_2$ she sees that the coin landed $head$ and therefore forms the belief that it landed $head$ and therefore agrees that the person who had the long side of the bet should be awarded the stake. (The belief, like Buchak and Owens say, is of deontological relevance in that S.B. would resent it if the person who held the short side of the bet was awarded the stake)
